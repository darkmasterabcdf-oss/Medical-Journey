# Glycolysis (The EMP Pathway)

## 1. Overview & Substrates
* **Definition:** An oxygen-independent respiratory pathway for monosaccharides.
* **Formal Name:** Known as the **EMP pathway**, named after the initials of the scientists who discovered it (Embden, Meyerhof, and Parnas).
* **Primary Substrate:** Glucose.
* **Alternative Substrate:** Fructose can also be utilized, provided the enzyme **fructokinase** is present.
    * *Location of Fructokinase:* Found mainly in the liver, with smaller amounts in the kidneys and intestines.

---

## 2. Hexose Transport into Cells
* **Mechanism:** Hexoses (glucose and fructose) move from the extracellular fluid into the intracellular fluid via specific transport proteins called **GLUT proteins**.
* **Types of GLUT Proteins:**
    * **Glucose transporters:** GLUT1, GLUT2, GLUT3, and GLUT4.
    * **Fructose transporters:** GLUT2 and GLUT5.
    * **GLUT4 Specifics:** Functions exclusively in muscle and adipose (fat) tissue and is **insulin-dependent**.
* **Driving Force (Equilibrium):** 
    * GLUT proteins are bidirectional transporters that establish a concentration equilibrium between the inside and outside of the cell.
    * To keep driving glucose *into* the cell, intracellular glucose must be continuously consumed. This reduces the intracellular "active mass" of glucose and shifts the equilibrium inward.

---

## 3. Steps of the Primary Glycolytic Pathway
1. **Glucose $\rightarrow$ Glucose-6-phosphate** *(Consumes 1 ATP)*
2. **Glucose-6-phosphate $\rightarrow$ Fructose-6-phosphate**
3. **Fructose-6-phosphate $\rightarrow$ Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate** *(Consumes 1 ATP)*
4. **Cleavage:** The enzyme **aldolase** splits fructose-1,6-bisphosphate into two 3-carbon molecules:
    * Dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP)
    * Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P)
    * *Note:* DHAP and G3P are interconvertible via the enzyme **triose-phosphate isomerase** (ultimately resulting in two molecules of G3P proceeding through the pathway).
5. **Oxidation & Phosphorylation:** $\text{NAD}^+$ oxidizes glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate and an inorganic phosphate group is added, forming **1,3-bisphosphoglycerate**.
    * *Yield:* Produces $2\text{ NADH} + \text{H}^+$ (one per G3P molecule).
6. **1,3-bisphosphoglycerate $\rightarrow$ 3-phosphoglycerate** *(Produces 2 ATP total)*
7. **Subsequent steps $\rightarrow$ Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) $\rightarrow$ Pyruvate** *(Produces 2 ATP total)*

---

## 4. Energy Accounting (Net Yield)
* **ATP Consumed:** **2 ATP** (at steps 1 and 3).
* **ATP Produced:** **4 ATP** (during the conversions of 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate to 3-phosphoglycerate, and phosphoenolpyruvate to pyruvate).
* **Net ATP Yield:** **2 ATP** ($4\text{ produced} - 2\text{ consumed}$).
* **Reducing Equivalents:** **2 $\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+$** (produced during the conversion of G3P to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate).

---

## 5. Alternative Pathway in RBCs: The Rapoport-Luebering Cycle
* **Context:** A secondary shunt pathway specific to Red Blood Cells (RBCs) that bypasses standard ATP generation.
* **Mechanism:**
    1. **1,3-bisphosphoglycerate** is converted into **2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG)** by *2,3-bisphosphoglycerate mutase*.
    2. **2,3-BPG** is converted directly into **3-phosphoglycerate** by *2,3-bisphosphoglycerate phosphatase*.
* **Impact on ATP:** Bypasses the ATP-generating step, meaning **2 ATP molecules are not formed** (net ATP yield becomes zero).
* **Physiological Significance:**
    * **Thermal Regulation:** Prevents RBCs from overheating when intracellular energy levels are already high.
    * **Glucose Uptake:** Continues consuming glucose to maintain the concentration gradient and facilitate glucose entry into the cell.
    * **Oxygen Delivery:** 2,3-BPG binds to hemoglobin and reduces its affinity for oxygen. At the tissue level, this promotes the release of oxygen from hemoglobin, increasing oxygen availability where it is needed most.

## 6. Fate of Pyruvate & $\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+$
