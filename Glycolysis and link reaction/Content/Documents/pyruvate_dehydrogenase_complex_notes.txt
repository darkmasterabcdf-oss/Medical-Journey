The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC) is a massive multi-enzyme complex that links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle by converting pyruvate into acetyl-CoA.

REQUIRED COENZYMES AND COFACTORS
* TPP (Thiamine pyrophosphate)
* Lipoamide (Lipoic acid covalently bound to a lysine residue)
* FAD (Flavin adenine dinucleotide)
* Coenzyme A (CoA-SH)
* $\text{NAD}^+$ (Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide)

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP-BY-STEP REACTION MECHANISM
1. Decarboxylation: 
   Pyruvate enters the complex and loses a carbon atom as $\text{CO}_2$, leaving behind a two-carbon hydroxyethyl group that binds to TPP.

2. Oxidation & Transfer: 
   The hydroxyethyl-TPP intermediate reacts with oxidized lipoamide, regenerating free TPP. Simultaneously:
   * The hydroxyethyl group (an alcohol) is oxidized into an acetyl group (a carbonyl).
   * The oxidized lipoamide is reduced to dihydrolipoamide as it accepts the acetyl group.

3. Acetyl-CoA Formation: 
   Coenzyme A attacks the acetyl-lipoamide intermediate, releasing acetyl-CoA and leaving behind fully reduced lipoamide.

4. Cofactor Regeneration: 
   To reset the complex for the next cycle:
   * Reduced lipoamide transfers its electrons and protons to FAD, forming $\text{FADH}_2$ and restoring oxidized lipoamide.
   * $\text{FADH}_2$ then passes those electrons to $\text{NAD}^+$, producing $\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+$.

------------------------------------------------------------

KEY STRUCTURAL FEATURES OF THE COMPLEX
* Massive Size: Has a total molecular weight of approximately $9 \times 10^6\text{ Da}$.
* Core Structure ($E_2$): Built around a structural core of 60 molecules of dihydrolipoyl transacetylase.
* Surrounding Enzymes ($E_1$ & $E_3$): Surrounded by 20 to 30 molecules each of pyruvate dehydrogenase ($E_1$) and dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase ($E_3$), which complete the complex.
